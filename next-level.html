The big one

Getting in front of high-end clients — and the people who look after them

The principle

You don’t advertise your way to this client. You get introduced.

Discretion is the product — so the way you grow has to match it. This isn’t billboards or boosted posts. It’s personal, selective, and quiet: a few warm introductions at a time, from people who already have this client’s trust. Done right, being “hard to find” becomes part of the appeal.

1
Start here · this week

Your own best clients

Your quietest, most trusted clients often are — or personally know — exactly the people you want to reach. The move isn’t a public “refer a friend” post. It’s a private, personal open door: quietly inviting one trusted client to introduce one person they’d vouch for, on their terms. Highest trust, costs nothing, and you can do it today.

2
The trusted circle around them

The people who already serve high-end clients

You reach the client by first becoming the trusted, discreet hair referral for the professionals already in their world — and offering to send your clients their way in return. Who to approach:

  • Personal stylists & image consultants
  • Makeup artists (especially bridal)
  • Luxury & med spas, aesthetic clinics
  • High-end wedding & event planners — your bridal strength
  • Luxury real-estate agents — they greet every new affluent household
  • Concierge desks at private clubs & country clubs
  • Personal assistants & estate managers who book for their people
  • Family offices & lifestyle-concierge firms
  • Charity gala & philanthropy committees
  • A local luxury boutique or two

How to actually find the assistants & concierges

  • Your clients are the bridge. Many high-end clients already have an assistant. The simplest way in is to ask a client you trust: “Who looks after your calendar — would you trust me with them?” That one question turns into a warm introduction, not a cold call.
  • You’re in the right place. Fairfield County — Greenwich and Westport especially — has one of the densest concentrations of family offices, private clubs, and lifestyle-concierge firms in the country. That’s literally where these assistants and concierges work. A short, specific list can be researched and worked through one at a time (there’s already a “Network Opportunities” tracker started for exactly this).
  • Luxury real-estate agents are the fastest warm door. They meet every new high-net-worth family moving into the area, and get asked “who do you recommend for…?” all the time. Be their answer for hair.
  • Get the name, not just the desk. A club’s member-services page or a quick LinkedIn look gives you the actual person’s name — so every note is personal and specific, never a mass mailing. (Personal is the whole point; a blast undoes it.)
